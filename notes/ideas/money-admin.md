# Money & Admin — 5 candidate ideas

Domain: bills, subscriptions, bank statements, tax, refunds, insurance, warranties,
government/council forms, receipts, price comparison, splitting costs, chasing money
owed, cancelling things, understanding letters and contracts.

Scouted 7 candidates, ran a prior-art hunt (Devpost/GitHub/Product Hunt/App
Store/Play Store) and a feasibility pass (against the actual starter kit) on all 7 in
parallel, then killed 3 on prior art (near-identical shipped apps) and added 1 new
candidate to reach 5. Killed: **payslip/tax-code decoder** (PaySlipIQ, PaySlipCheck,
TaxDigit already do photo → plain-English decode + tax-code check), **PCN appeal
drafter** (Appeal My PCN already does "photo PCN → tailored appeal letter in 30
seconds," exact match), **return-window/warranty countdown** (Warranty Keeper,
ReceiptSnap, Reclaim, Claimly all do photo receipt → countdown → alert already,
saturated category).

Ranked best first.

```
IDEA 1 — Deadline Priority Inbox
Problem in one sentence: I have four unopened letters on the counter and no idea which one actually matters.
Who and how often:            Every UK adult who lives with any paperwork (bills, council letters, insurance notices, subscription emails) accumulates several with real deadlines each week; the problem isn't reading one letter, it's triaging a pile of them under time pressure.
The 90-second wow:            Judge hands over (or the team pre-stages) 3-4 different bills/letters — a red final-notice-style utility bill, a bland subscription renewal, an urgent-looking scam text screenshot, a council tax reminder. Upload all four at once; in one call the app returns a single ranked list: "1. Pay this by Thursday or a debt collector gets involved (£85) — 2. This one's just a subscription renewal, ignore until next month — 3. This is probably fake, don't act on it." The judge visibly reorders their own sense of urgency.
Google feature named out loud: Gemini 3.7 Flash agentic multi-document reasoning in a single multi-image tool call — the model has to read four unrelated documents, extract a deadline and consequence from each, then rank them against each other. A single-document chatbot can't do the cross-document ranking step; this needs genuine multi-step reasoning.
Closest existing thing:       Single-bill reminder/tracker apps and generic "how to write a late-payment email" guides (e.g. Chaser, Wollit blog content) exist, but no shipped product does multi-document batch upload + consequence-severity ranking + a single £-prioritized action list. Web search across Devpost/GitHub/App Store/Play Store found no direct match — this is real whitespace among the seven candidates researched. — Delta: the ranking-by-consequence step itself, not document Q&A.
Build in 3h:                  Files: `app.py` (Streamlit/Flask upload UI), `deadline_inbox.py` (Gemini call), `utils.py` (reused from starter — `get_client()`, `with_retry()`), `schema.py` (Pydantic model: items[], each with deadline/consequence/£amount, plus ranked[] order), 3-4 sourced sample letter images staged in `sample_data/`. Uses the `client.files.upload()` pattern from `docs/04-gemini-agent-api.md` for each image, then one `interactions.create()` call with all four file references + the JSON schema. Riskiest 20 minutes: combining multiple uploaded files into one `interactions.create` call is not demonstrated anywhere in the starter kit — spike this exact call shape first, before building any UI, since the whole idea depends on it working.
When the API throttles:        This is the cheapest RPM profile of the batch — one call per full demo run, not one per document — so it's the least likely candidate to hit 15 RPM. If it does, or wifi drops: pre-cache the ranked JSON output for the staged demo documents and serve it from a `DEMO_OFFLINE` flag; fall back to the recorded video.
Quotable number:              Sorting a pile of bills by what actually matters: 20 minutes of dread and re-reading → one ranked list in under 10 seconds.
Which track it fits:          agents / productivity
Kill risk:                    The multi-file-in-one-call input shape is untested in the provided starter kit — if it silently fails or the model can't cleanly attribute claims to the right source document, the whole demo breaks live. Must be proven working in the first 20 minutes, before anything else is built.
```

```
IDEA 2 — Owed Money Message Coach
Problem in one sentence: My flatmate owes me £50 and every message I draft either sounds too aggressive or too weak, so I never send it and just eat the cost.
Who and how often:            Informal debts (split bills, fronted concert tickets, "I'll pay you back Friday") are extremely common among adults living with roommates, friends, or family, and the specific moment of "I need to ask for this back without ruining the relationship" recurs multiple times a week across an ordinary social/financial life — not a rare event.
The 90-second wow:            Pure judge-supplied live input, no props needed: the judge just says a made-up scenario out loud ("my friend borrowed £340 three months ago for a flight, we're still close, I've mentioned it once already"). The app takes relationship, amount, time elapsed, and prior-reminder count, and returns three calibrated message options (gentle nudge / clear ask / firm final request) plus a visible escalation ladder ("if no reply in 5 days, send message 2"). The judge picks one and it reads exactly right for the scenario they just invented.
Google feature named out loud: Gemini 3.7 Flash's agentic reasoning across multiple weighted variables at once (relationship closeness, amount, elapsed time, reminder history) to produce a genuinely different output each time, plus a visible multi-step escalation plan — not a single fixed template with a name swapped in.
Closest existing thing:       Splitwise and similar expense-splitting apps (Splitwise, Tab, Settle Up) track who-owes-what and have a basic "Remind" button, but it sends one fixed, generic notification — it does not draft tone-calibrated, escalation-aware messages tailored to the relationship and history. This candidate was not run through the formal prior-art web search alongside the other six (added late, after the search round) — flagging that explicitly rather than claiming full clearance; the assessment above is based on direct knowledge of Splitwise/Venmo/Tab's actual reminder features, which are template-fixed, not AI-drafted. — Delta: AI-calibrated tone and a planned escalation sequence, not a fixed reminder button.
Build in 3h:                  Files: `app.py` (simple form: relationship, amount, weeks elapsed, prior reminders sent — or free-text scenario box), `message_coach.py` (Gemini call), `utils.py` (reused), `schema.py` (Pydantic model: tone, message_variants[3], escalation_ladder[]). Text-only, following the same `interactions.create()` structured-output pattern as `04_structured_output.py` in the starter kit — no image upload needed, so no dependency on the untested file-upload call shape. Riskiest 20 minutes: prompt engineering so the three message variants sound genuinely different in register rather than the same sentence reworded — worth writing 2-3 few-shot examples into the prompt rather than trusting zero-shot.
When the API throttles:        Cheapest possible profile: one text-only call per demo, easily within 15 RPM. If wifi dies: this is the one candidate across the whole shortlist plausible enough to run on-device — Gemma 4 text-only local fallback, per the feasibility check, is realistic here since there's no image/vision dependency. Otherwise, pre-cache 2-3 example scenario outputs and fall back to the recorded video.
Quotable number:              An awkward £50 debt that sat silent for six weeks: 20 minutes of drafting-and-deleting a text → a message that actually gets sent in 15 seconds.
Which track it fits:          agents / productivity
Kill risk:                    Without the visible escalation-ladder step on screen, this can read as "just a text generator" rather than something agentic — the pitch must show the multi-step plan, not just one message, or it looks like a thin wrapper.
```

```
IDEA 3 — Renewal Ambush Negotiator
Problem in one sentence: My insurance/gym/streaming renewal came in at a higher price than last year and I don't know if that's normal or if I'm being taken for a ride.
Who and how often:            A typical UK household holds something like 8-10 active subscriptions and policies (streaming, insurance, gym, phone, broadband, etc.), each renewing on its own schedule — across the whole household basket, a price-increase or renewal notice lands roughly weekly, even though any single policy only renews once a year. Framed at the household-basket level, not the single-policy level.
The 90-second wow:            Paste or photograph a renewal notice (real or one the team stages); the app extracts last year's price and this year's price, computes the % hike, and — instead of just a comparison chart — outputs a verbatim phone script: "Hi, I've been a customer for 3 years, I've seen [X] offering the same cover for £[Y], can you match that or I'll need to switch." The judge can read the script out loud on the spot.
Google feature named out loud: Gemini 3.7 Flash agentic tool-calling — extract structured numbers from unstructured text/image, then compose natural persuasive language grounded in those numbers, in one pipeline.
Closest existing thing:       insurance.contextinit.com (an "Insurance Policy Premium Negotiator" tool, requires manual quote upload + a paid Claude Pro subscription) plus static advice content (renewaltrap.com, CheckMyOverpay blog posts on "how to call your insurer"). None of these do live photo-or-paste-in → automatic % hike calculation → verbatim script output as one seamless pipeline; the closest is a manual, paid, upload-based tool, not a live camera-to-script flow. — Delta: the live photo-in-script-out pipeline, and no login/payment required.
Build in 3h:                  Files: `app.py`, `renewal_negotiator.py`, `utils.py` (reused), `schema.py` (Pydantic model: old_price, new_price, pct_hike, script), `sample_data/renewal.txt` (synthetic sample renewal notice for the demo). Text-only `interactions.create()` call, same structured-output pattern as the starter kit's `04_structured_output.py`. Riskiest 20 minutes: the "typical market range" comparison needs an honest decision up front — either wire real Search grounding (the starter kit's `03_grounded_agent.py` pattern) or clearly label it as an illustrative estimate in the UI; deciding this mid-build wastes time.
When the API throttles:        Lowest-risk candidate on RPM: one call per demo run. If it throttles or wifi dies, this is the other candidate plausible on local Gemma 4 (text-only, no vision dependency) per the feasibility check; otherwise pre-cache the sample output and fall back to video.
Quotable number:               A quietly inflated renewal: a 34% "loyalty penalty" price hike hidden in the small print → one 20-second script that gets it back toward last year's price.
Which track it fits:          agents / productivity
Kill risk:                    If the "market range" number isn't genuinely grounded (real search or a real public dataset), a sharp judge can ask "where did that number come from" and expose it as invented — must either ground it for real or disclaim it clearly rather than presenting it as fact.
```

```
IDEA 4 — Statement Fee Hunter
Problem in one sentence: I know I'm paying for subscriptions I forgot about, but I don't want to link my actual bank account to another app to find out which ones.
Who and how often:            Anyone who checks a bank or card statement — which most adults do multiple times a week via their banking app — has this exact "wait, what's this charge" moment regularly; UK survey data (Barclaycard/Which?) puts average forgotten-subscription waste at several hundred pounds a year per household.
The 90-second wow:            Paste in a statement (real, anonymized, or a sample the judge dictates: "there's a £9.99 charge every month called 'Streamly', a £45 one called 'FitPass', and a random £2.50 fee") — the app clusters the recurring charges, names each forgotten subscription, flags the anomalous fee, computes "you're bleeding £57.49/month," and drafts a ready-to-send cancellation message for each one.
Google feature named out loud: Gemini 3.7 Flash multi-step reasoning over an unstructured transaction list — clustering repeated-but-differently-formatted charges into named subscriptions and separating genuine anomalies from normal spending is a reasoning task, not a regex match.
Closest existing thing:       Rocket Money and Trim — large, established products that already do recurring-charge clustering, "you're bleeding £X," and one-tap cancellation, via OAuth/Plaid bank-linking. — Delta: this candidate explicitly has no OAuth or bank login — it works from a pasted or photographed statement instead, which is both a genuine privacy angle and the only version of this idea that's actually demoable live on a judge's laptop without a real bank login. The core value proposition is not new (Rocket Money owns it), so the pitch has to lead hard with the no-login wedge, not the "find my subscriptions" wedge.
Build in 3h:                  Files: `app.py`, `fee_hunter.py`, `utils.py` (reused), `schema.py` (Pydantic model: recurring_charges[], anomalies[], monthly_waste, cancellation_drafts[]), `sample_data/statement.txt` (a hand-written synthetic statement with obvious repeated charges — real bank data risks free-tier training-use terms per `docs/07-setup-keys-quotas-cost.md` §3.4, so don't use a real one). Text-only `interactions.create()` call, same structured-output pattern as the kit's `04_structured_output.py`. Riskiest 20 minutes: the model mis-clustering charges on messy real-world formatting — mitigate by using a clean, obviously-repeating synthetic statement for the live demo rather than trusting arbitrary live-pasted input to parse well on stage.
When the API throttles:        One call per demo, safe within 15 RPM. If wifi dies: pre-cache the sample statement's output JSON and serve it offline; no realistic on-device fallback identified for this one (text volume is fine for Gemma 4, but wasn't specifically load-tested) — recorded video is the honest fallback.
Quotable number:              The average household is quietly paying for subscriptions it forgot about — around £300+ a year, per UK consumer surveys — found and named in under 10 seconds.
Which track it fits:          productivity / agents
Kill risk:                    Rocket Money is a well-known name; if a judge says "isn't this just Rocket Money," the answer has to be instant and confident ("Rocket Money needs your bank login, this needs nothing") or the idea reads as a weaker clone rather than a deliberate privacy-first alternative.
```

```
IDEA 5 — Scam / Fake Bill & Letter Detector
Problem in one sentence: I got a text/letter about a parcel fee or a council tax "final notice" and I can't tell if it's real or someone trying to rob me.
Who and how often:            UK adults report encountering multiple scam texts, emails, or letters per week (Ofcom/Action Fraud reporting patterns); this is one of the most frequent adversarial-document encounters an ordinary adult has, arguably more often than any other candidate here.
The 90-second wow:            Judge hands over or photographs a fake or ambiguous bill/letter live (the team stages a genuinely convincing fake alongside a real one as a control). The app analyzes urgency language, payment-method plausibility (a real council never asks for bank transfer to a personal account), reference-number format, and layout inconsistencies, then returns a scored verdict with the specific red flags listed in plain English — "this asks for payment via bank transfer within 24 hours, real HMRC/council notices never do that."
Google feature named out loud: Gemini 3.7 Flash multimodal image analysis combined with multi-step reasoning (read the text, check it against known legitimate-sender patterns, weigh multiple red flags into one verdict) — or Gemma 4 on-device as the privacy-first version, since a person's actual suspicious mail never has to leave their phone.
Closest existing thing:       Scam Scanner (Play Store) and ScamScan (App Store, "AI scam detector") both already do "photograph a letter → AI scam verdict with red flags" as shipped consumer apps. — Delta: this candidate is deliberately narrowed to UK-specific bill/letter formats (council tax, TV licence, parcel-fee texts, "final notice" utility bills) with pattern checks specific to those document types, rather than generic scam detection — a real but thin delta; the pitch must lean hard on document-type specificity or it reads as a duplicate of an app that already exists.
Build in 3h:                  Files: `app.py`, `scam_detector.py`, `utils.py` (reused), `schema.py` (Pydantic model: score, verdict, red_flags[] — text list, not bounding boxes), 3-4 sourced sample letter/text-screenshot images (one genuine fake, one real control, staged in `sample_data/`). Uses `client.files.upload()` + one `interactions.create()` call with the file reference and the JSON schema. Riskiest 20 minutes: this candidate needs the same untested combined image+text input call as Idea 1, and pixel-accurate red-flag overlay coordinates on the image are unreliable from a text model — cut scope to a clearly labeled text list of red flags rather than an on-image overlay, decided up front rather than discovered mid-build.
When the API throttles:        No realistic offline-vision fallback exists in the timeframe (Gemma 4 on-device vision was measured at ~4.7 tokens/sec with a ~65s cold load in feasibility testing — too slow for a live demo beat). Pre-cache 3-4 verdicts as JSON for the staged sample documents and serve them via a `DEMO_OFFLINE` flag if the live call fails; recorded video is the real fallback.
Quotable number:              A scam bill built to be paid in a 3-minute panic → caught, named, and explained in under 10 seconds, before a card ever comes out.
Which track it fits:          safety / on-device / accessibility
Kill risk:                    This is the most crowded category researched — at least two near-identical shipped apps exist — combined with the riskiest, least-tested technical dependency (multimodal file input) of the five. If a judge has seen a scam-detector app before, the pitch needs the UK-document specificity front and center in the first 10 seconds, not left implicit.
```

## Ranking rationale (best first)

1. **Deadline Priority Inbox** — cleanest prior-art whitespace found in the whole search (no direct shipped match), genuinely needs multi-document agentic reasoning (not fakeable with a script), and has the safest API-call profile. Main risk is a technical unknown (multi-file input), not a market one.
2. **Owed Money Message Coach** — the single best "judge supplies live input" moment of all five (needs zero props, just a sentence), cheapest and safest build (text-only, no vision dependency), and a real behavioral gap next to Splitwise's fixed-template reminder button. Added late in the process and not run through the formal prior-art search alongside the other six — flagged honestly above.
3. **Renewal Ambush Negotiator** — lowest technical risk in the whole set (text-only, one call, plausible on-device fallback), clear delta (live photo-to-script vs. a paid manual-upload competitor), strong quotable number, but the "market range" comparison must be genuinely grounded or disclaimed or it's a fabrication risk on stage.
4. **Statement Fee Hunter** — real and defensible no-OAuth privacy delta, but sits in the shadow of a very well-known incumbent (Rocket Money); wins only if the pitch leads with "no bank login" in the first sentence.
5. **Scam / Fake Bill & Letter Detector** — the most dramatic possible wow moment and the domain's single highest weekly-frequency claim, but it's the most crowded category found (two close shipped matches) and carries the riskiest unverified technical dependency (combined image+text input, unreliable overlay) — rank last despite the strong story.

Candidates killed on prior art: **Payslip & Tax Code Decoder** (PaySlipIQ, PaySlipCheck, TaxDigit already ship this exact photo→decode flow), **PCN Appeal Drafter** (Appeal My PCN already does "photo PCN → tailored appeal letter in 30 seconds," an exact match), **Return-Window & Warranty Countdown** (Warranty Keeper, ReceiptSnap, Reclaim, Claimly all ship near-identical photo-receipt-to-countdown mechanics — saturated).
