The decision, in one paragraph

At 14:30 spend twenty minutes and nothing more proving one thing: that gemma4:e4b in Ollama will accept an image and return a short structured answer on this laptop. Everything else follows from that single result. If it works, build Blind-Spot Wayfinder on Track 2 (Best Use of Gemma) — photograph a posted map, ask which exit, answered with the network off. Its novelty is verified clean, offline is the premise rather than a fallback, and it runs the Ollama path. If it fails, drop it without argument and build Spot the Gap on Track 1 — pure text, the one proven starter pattern, no image risk anywhere. Do not attempt both. Do not spend the afternoon rescuing the spike.

Revised at 13:38 — the novelty judge killed the original pick

This page first recommended Overshare Check, with Second Look as the fallback. Both were downgraded within minutes of publishing, and the honest version of the page keeps the record rather than quietly rewriting it.

  • Second Look — KILLED, novelty 2. Underpriced AI already ships photos or screenshots from any marketplace into sold comps, a deal score and red flags; Spottable does the same for Marketplace. The buyer-versus-reseller framing was the only unshipped sliver, and a judge will not credit it.
  • Overshare Check — survives at novelty 3, demoted. Lookr already detects and blurs private classes in photos. The genuinely new move is the inference chain — "what a stranger could do with this" — not the detection. The category is warm, not cold.
  • A second, separate problem with it: Overshare Check runs Gemma 4 in-browser through MediaPipe and WebGPU, not through Ollama. The measured numbers and the correction below apply to the Ollama path only. The in-browser stack is unverified end to end, and so are ContextCrop and Which Button, which share it.

The pattern behind all of this: four of eight domain scouts had their own top pick killed on prior art they claimed to have checked — Bottle Cam, Is This Real?, Notification Declutter Coach and Second Look. Self-verified novelty was unreliable across the board. Whatever you build, re-check it yourself first.

Two corrections that changed this ranking

There is no public-URL requirement. The brief every scouting agent worked from said "a localhost demo scores as broken." That was inferred from other Google events and it is wrong for this one. Actual submission: public GitHub repo with README, architecture diagram and MIT/Apache licence · a 2-minute demo video on Loom or YouTube · a 2–3 paragraph write-up. docs/10-tracks-rules-rubric.md

This rescues the on-device ideas. A recorded video means you control the take: pre-warm the model, cut the cold load, retry until clean. The measured 4.74 tok/s and 65-second cold load stop being stage-killers and become production constraints. Ideas my own demo judge downgraded for live-failure risk move back up.

Real rubric: 30% technical execution and model leverage · 25% innovation and originality · 25% real-world impact and UX · 20% presentation, with demo reliability named explicitly. Deep use of one feature scores above shallow use of several.

The twenty-minute spike that decides everything

Nothing in this repo proves a local image call works. starter/07_local_gemma.py is text-only over Ollama; the images=[...] parameter is demonstrated nowhere. The capability is real — docs/05-gemma-4-on-device.md:51 lists E2B and E4B as Text, Image, Audio with a ~150M-parameter vision encoder — but the call shape and the speed are unproven on this hardware. That is the risk, not whether the model can see.

ollama run gemma4:e4b ""          # pre-warm, kills the 65s cold load
# then: one image + "list what is readable in this photo, max 4 short items"
# pass/fail in 20 minutes. No extensions.

Pass condition: a short structured answer about a real photo, in under 12 seconds, warm. Anything else is a fail — including "it worked but took 40 seconds."

The ninety seconds — staging you should copy

This was written for Overshare Check, which is now demoted. It stays because the staging is the best in the corpus and transfers directly to Blind-Spot Wayfinder: airplane mode switched on in shot, the viewer chooses what to photograph, the airplane icon stays on screen throughout, findings appear one at a time as chains rather than labels, and the closing line points at the network that never came back on. Swap "what a stranger would learn from this photo" for "which exit you need", and the beats hold.

Opening line: "Put your phone in airplane mode and take a photo of wherever you're standing — I want to show you what a stranger would learn from it."

0:00–0:08Airplane mode switched on on camera, phone held up so the icon is visible. Opening line.
0:08–0:15One photo — the table, a badge, a whiteboard, whatever is there.
0:15–0:28Screen reads "Reading locally. No network." The airplane icon stays visible throughout.
0:28–0:50Four findings appear one at a time, each as a chain rather than a label: "Badge, bottom left → your full name and employer." "Whiteboard → the venue wifi password." "Screen behind you → an open inbox, three sender names readable."
0:50–1:00One tap blurs all four regions. Before/after toggle, twice.
1:00–1:12"The wifi never came back on. Nothing you just photographed left this phone."
1:12–1:30The number, then one line on what ships next.

The quotable number: a photo you would have posted without thinking → four things a stranger could learn about you, caught before you post, with the network off. Measure the real seconds at 16:00 and use the true figure. Do not say "8 seconds" until a stopwatch says so.

Never claim it catches everything. Say "here's what I can see so far" — that turns a miss into a stated limit instead of a failure.

Ideas worth stealing from, but not building

The evidence thread

From Packet Cross-Exam: a line drawn from a claim to the exact piece of evidence that supports or contradicts it. The best "make the reasoning visible" device anyone proposed, and it fits whatever you build. Model leverage is 30% of the score and this is how you show it.

Let the judge set the win condition

From Watchword: the viewer invents the trigger, performs it themselves, and the payoff is binary — it fires or it does not. Unfakeable. The idea itself is unbuildable at 4.74 tok/s; the shape is free.

Bottle Cam — the trap

Near-perfect judge-supplied moment attached to the thinnest product here. Against a rubric weighting model leverage at 30% and innovation at 25%, "Gemini reads an image" scores almost nothing. A great party trick that loses on the scorecard.

Big Font — check for a printer

Its whole payoff is handing someone a printed A4 sheet. There is unlikely to be a printer at Imperial. Without the print it is a nicely typeset page on a screen, a full grade lower.
